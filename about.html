Hi, I'm Jany

I teach Taiwan Mandarin to learners from beginner to advanced, with over 1,000 hours of teaching experience. I hold TCSOL and TQUK teaching certifications, and in 2024 I spent a year in New Zealand. I love sharing Chinese culture, and I bring it into every lesson.

Why “Oops!”?

“Oops!” means two things to me. It sounds like my family name, 吳 (Wú), and Taiwanese people love a good pun. It's also how I want you to feel about mistakes: they're normal, and nothing to be afraid of.

As a child, I was very afraid of making mistakes, especially the red-pen circles on my homework. As I grew up, I learned that everyone makes mistakes, and each one simply shows you what to fix next time. This is a message for my learners, and for myself too.

How I teach

If you freeze mid-sentence, I'll say: just try, say what you remember. If the word is really gone, I'll remind you of what we talked about last time. I remember what my students say, and a little context often brings the word back. Forgetting isn't a failure. It just means we need a better way to remember.

I love pictures, stories and games. One student told me he'll never forget 遠 (far) and 近 (near): to travel far, you need lots of clothes (see 衣 at the bottom of 遠), and an axe, which looks like the shape in 近, can only chop things nearby. It's a memory trick, not etymology, but now he sees the picture every time.

I'm also a big Detective Conan fan, so I treat every student like a little mystery: how does this person learn best?

Lessons built around you

I don't hand you a fixed course. I get to know how you learn, and we design your lessons together.

  • We start with a conversation. In your trial lesson, I learn your goals and how you like to be taught.
  • Every lesson adapts to you. On an energetic day, we cover more vocabulary and sentence patterns. If you're tired, I slow down right away and keep things light.
  • Structure, if you like structure. I can recommend a textbook and follow it with you, or build lessons around your goals.
  • Interaction, if you like to talk. We learn through conversations and games.
  • No two students learn the same way. Some follow a textbook, some read comics together, and some practice everyday conversation using objects around us.
  • Support after class. I can provide handouts or homework whenever you'd like them.

I'm a highly sensitive person, and that helps me notice how you're doing and adjust right away, so your lessons stay relaxed and useful.

What a lesson feels like

Most of my students are adults with busy, stressful jobs. I don't want lessons to feel like pressure, or to leave you sad about words you couldn't remember. I want you to learn Mandarin while you're relaxed, almost without noticing, and then use it in real life.
